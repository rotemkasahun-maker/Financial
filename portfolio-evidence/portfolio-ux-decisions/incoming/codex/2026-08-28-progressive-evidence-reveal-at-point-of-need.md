# Incoming Decision Candidate — Progressive Evidence at the Point of Need

1. **Decision title:** Reveal evidence progressively at the moment it becomes meaningful.
2. **Approximate date / phase:** 2026-08-23–27; partial-reveal studies through opening roadmap.
3. **Context / trigger:** Dense evidence and full screens risked cognitive overload, weak crops, and proof disconnected from the story.
4. **User problem:** `DESIGN INFERENCE`: viewers need overview-to-depth control and proof when its meaning is available, not an evidence dump.
5. **Previous assumption/state:** Full screenshots/carousels could be placed as static proof blocks.
6. **Constraints:** Keep screenshots sharp; preserve context; avoid hiding essential evidence behind interaction.
7. **Alternatives considered:** Full screen immediately; carousel; detail → source → payoff → next detail → trace.
8. **Rejected approaches:** Blurry carousel proof, random crops, too many full phone frames, and evidence appearing before the thought.
9. **Trade-offs:** Lower initial proof density and clearer pacing, but some viewers may not reach deferred detail.
10. **Final decision:** Introduce a meaningful crop/artifact, then reveal its source/context when the narrative needs it.
11. **Resulting logic:** Evidence arrival follows the collection thought; partial-reveal prototypes open a detail into the real Attention screen.
12. **Edge cases:** Inline evidence must remain understandable without motion; essential proof should not require an undiscoverable action.
13. **Viewer value:** Reduces overload while preserving depth and context.
14. **Portfolio/design impact:** Shot selection, crop states, and motion order become part of information architecture.
15. **USER-STATED NEED:** Documented preference not to over-explain and to use compact proof; no viewer-stated need.
16. **DESIGN INFERENCE:** Progressive disclosure supports scanning and deeper inspection.
